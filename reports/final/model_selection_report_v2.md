## 2. Selection

**Protocol v2** (constants in `src/config.py`):

* 0. Candidates = models with probability outputs (8); benchmarks only: Persistence (same as last month), Seasonal naive (same month last year).
* a. Ranked by mean macro-F1 on selection folds 4-6 (Jan-Jun 2024): best = LightGBM (0.5948).
* b. Tie band 0.005: tied models = LightGBM (0.5948).
* c. Most stable (lowest std on folds 4-6), then cheapest: SELECTED = LightGBM (std 0.0561, fit 0.6s).

**Transparency: why the protocol was revised.** A first run (v1, kept in `model_selection_report_v1.md`) ranked *all* models, rules included, by mean macro-F1 over all 6 folds and selected *Seasonal naive*. Three methodological flaws explain that choice:
1. In folds 1-3 (Jul-Dec 2023) there is no 2022 data, so seasonal naive silently falls back to persistence: its fold scores there are *identical* to persistence, so its 6-fold mean mixes two methods.
2. CatBoost/LightGBM had been tuned on the same folds used for selection, which inflates their scores.
3. Rules output a single class, not probabilities, but the system must rank plants by risk and show probabilities in the front end. They are benchmarks, not deployable candidates.
The flaws are methodological and do not depend on test results, but the revision was made **after** v1's test results had been seen. This is stated openly as a limitation.
