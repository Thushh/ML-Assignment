# Model selection report (final assignment)

Generated by `src/final_train.py` (protocol v3).

## 1. Use case and what 'best' means

CEB planners need, before each month, the expected output tier (Low / Moderate / High) of every renewable plant.
A good model must (1) classify all tiers, including the minority **Low** tier (under-delivery) -> **macro-F1**; (2) give trustworthy **probabilities** to rank plants by risk -> **log-loss**, ROC-AUC; (3) beat 'same as last month' where it matters -> **tier-change accuracy**.

## 2. Evaluation design: rolling origin over 2024

Each month of 2024 is predicted by models trained on **all earlier months** (3,899 predictions, 12 months, a full monsoon cycle). CatBoost/LightGBM were tuned on Jul-Dec 2023 only. The hold-out row (Kirkoswald, Dec-2024) is excluded everywhere.

## 7. How the evaluation protocol evolved (transparency)

* **v1** (`model_selection_report_v1.md`): all models incl. rules ranked on 6 expanding folds; picked *seasonal naive*, the worst model on Jul-Dec 2024. Flaws: seasonal naive silently equals persistence where no previous year exists; tuned models were scored on their own tuning folds; rules give no probabilities.
* **v2** (`model_selection_report_v2.md`): tuning folds separated from selection folds (Jan-Jun 2024), rules as benchmarks; picked *LightGBM*, which Jul-Dec 2024 then showed to be significantly worse than several models. Cause: three 2-month folds cannot separate models whose differences (~0.01) are far smaller than the fold-to-fold noise (~0.05), and a fixed tie band of 0.005 ignored that uncertainty.
* **v3** (this report): the use case defines 'best'; all 12 months of 2024 are evaluated by rolling origin; ties are decided by a significance test, not a fixed margin. Trade-off, stated openly: with only 24 months of data there is no untouched test period left after v3, and v3 was designed after v1/v2 results had been seen.
